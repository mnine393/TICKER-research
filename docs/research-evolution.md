### Tesla, Inc. (TSLA) — Company Research Report

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026. **Source: UNSOURCED — assignment input.**  
**Scope:** Filing-based preliminary research; no price target. **Source: UNSOURCED — author scope.**  
**Primary document:** Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2025, filed January 29, 2026, accession `0001628280-26-003952`. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Cover page, p. 1.**

#### Executive summary

Tesla is a Texas corporation whose common stock trades on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under `TSLA`. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Cover page, p. 1.** The company reports two operating and reportable segments: automotive and energy generation and storage. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 2.**

The investment case has conflicting signals. Automotive revenue declined in 2025, while energy-generation-and-storage revenue grew and Tesla reported substantial cash and short-term investments. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 50; Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 49.** Tesla also expects 2026 capital expenditures to exceed $20 billion. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, MD&A—2026 Outlook, p. 32.** These cross-currents support further research rather than an immediate position decision. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

#### Business overview

Tesla's automotive segment includes the design, development, manufacturing, sales, and leasing of electric vehicles, as well as automotive regulatory credits; the energy-generation-and-storage segment includes the design, manufacture, installation, sales, and leasing of energy products and related services. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 2–3; Note 16, Segment Reporting, p. 97.** Powerwall is designed for homes and small commercial facilities, while Megapack serves commercial, industrial, utility, and energy-generation customers. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 3.**

Tesla also describes investments in AI-enabled products and services, including Robotaxi and Optimus. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, MD&A—2026 Outlook, pp. 31–32.** The timing, economics, and probability of commercial success for those products remain unresolved in this analysis. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author uncertainty.**

#### 2025 operating performance

Total revenue was $94.827 billion in 2025, down from $97.690 billion in 2024. Automotive revenue was $69.526 billion, down 10% from $77.070 billion, and energy-generation-and-storage revenue was $12.771 billion, up 27% from $10.086 billion. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 50; Item 7, MD&A—Results of Operations, pp. 34–37.**

Tesla reported 46.7 GWh of energy-storage deployments in 2025. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, MD&A—2025 Highlights, p. 31.** The company attributed the energy segment's 2025 revenue growth primarily to higher Megapack and Powerwall deployments, partly offset by a lower Megapack average selling price. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, MD&A—Energy Generation and Storage Segment, p. 37.** Energy-storage deployments and related revenue can be evaluated as a distinct enterprise-value driver rather than presumed to be offset by weaker automotive economics. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

Automotive regulatory-credit revenue was $1.993 billion in 2025. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 50.** Regulatory credits should be separated from recurring automotive operating performance when developing normalized margins. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

#### Liquidity, debt, and claim hierarchy

At December 31, 2025, Tesla reported $16.513 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $27.546 billion of short-term investments. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 49; Note 4, Cash and Cash Equivalents, p. 70.** The filing reported $8.177 billion of unpaid debt principal, consisting of $3 million of recourse debt and $8.174 billion of non-recourse debt. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Note 9—Debt, pp. 73–74.** Debt and finance leases had a $8.376 billion net carrying value, split between $1.640 billion current and $6.736 billion long term. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 49; Note 9—Debt, p. 73.**

The balance sheet also reported $58 million of redeemable noncontrolling interests and $670 million of noncontrolling interests. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 49.** A future enterprise-to-equity bridge should explicitly distinguish cash, short-term investments, debt, finance leases, noncontrolling interests, redeemable noncontrolling interests, and other debt-like or non-common claims. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.** Whether short-term investments are fully non-operating, and the appropriate treatment of leases and noncontrolling interests, remain open questions. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author uncertainty.**

#### Reinvestment and valuation readiness

Tesla expects 2026 capital expenditures to exceed $20 billion, driven by AI initiatives, compute infrastructure and data centers, manufacturing and R&D lines and facilities, AI-enabled fleet assets, and its retail, service, and charging footprint. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, MD&A—Cash Flow and Capital Expenditure Trends, p. 32.** That expected level of capex is material to reinvestment and free-cash-flow forecasts. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.** The split between maintenance and growth capex, and its timing, is not established here. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author uncertainty.**

The FY2025 diluted weighted-average share count was 3.528 billion, comprising 3.225 billion basic weighted-average shares and 303 million stock-based awards. **Source: Tesla 2025 Form 10-K, Note 4—Earnings per Share, p. 61.** That figure is an initial convention only and must be validated against a valuation-date share-count approach before it is used to calculate per-share value. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

This report does not treat the class synthetic enterprise-to-equity exercise as a Tesla valuation. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.** Any company-specific valuation should use FCFF and enterprise value before translating to common equity value, with a consistent bridge policy. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

#### Work required before an initiation decision

The next stage should reconcile the balance sheet, debt disclosures, and noncontrolling-interest disclosures into a single claim hierarchy; develop a five-year FCFF forecast; separate recurring results from regulatory credits and nonrecurring or classification effects; and assess DCF, reverse-DCF, and comparable-company ranges. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author research plan.** The decision should be tested under downside cases for automotive revenue, energy-storage growth, operating margin, capital expenditures, and cost of capital. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author research plan.**

The September 1, 2026 valuation date is after the December 31, 2025 financial-statement date. **Source: UNSOURCED — valuation-date input and date comparison.** Current market price, current capital structure, and a valuation-date share count are therefore not established by the cited 2025 Form 10-K alone. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author uncertainty.**

#### Investment Decision

**Watch / defer.** Do not initiate a Tesla position on this filing-only analysis; the evidence supports deeper valuation work, not a current position decision. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**

This call would change if updated valuation-date evidence and a completed, downside-tested FCFF and enterprise-to-equity bridge supported a position decision. **Source: UNSOURCED — preserved author judgment.**
